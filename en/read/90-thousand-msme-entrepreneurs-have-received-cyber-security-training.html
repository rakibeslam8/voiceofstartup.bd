90 thousand MSME entrepreneurs have received cyber security training

By VOSB Desk7 November 2026 at 8:53 pm3 min read

Asia Foundation and Sajeda Foundation have conducted nationwide training programs to raise awareness about cyber security. More than 90 thousand entrepreneurs have been trained in cyber hygiene under the program since July 2024.

This information was given in an event titled 'CyberReady: Celebrating the Journey of Cyber-Safe MSME' in Dhaka today. The speakers said that micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs) are gradually becoming dependent on digital technology. As a result, it is important to ensure cyber security for these organizations. Cyber ​​attacks, phishing and fraudulent transactions can cause small businesses to lose revenue, lose relationships with customers, and even risk going out of business. Therefore, cyber security should not be viewed as a technical issue; It is now also an important adjunct to economic development.

The event was organized to mark the completion of a project titled 'APAC Cyber ​​Security Fund'. The Asia Foundation manages the APAC Cyber ​​Security Fund with financial support from search engine Google's philanthropic organization Google.org. The local partner of the project is Sajeda Foundation. They informed the matter in a press release.

At the event, Asia Foundation Country Representative Kazi Faisal Bin Siraj said that protection from fraud and exploitation is necessary in the digital world as in real life.

Anthea Mulakala, Senior Director of Asia Foundation's Digital Economy Program, said cyber hygiene plays a role in protecting people's livelihoods, creating new opportunities and increasing trust in the digital system. He highlighted the experience of the project in Bangladesh.

Cyber ​​hygiene is the practice of following rules and procedures to keep digital devices, networks and sensitive data safe from cyber threats.

Senior Director of Sajeda Foundation. Nomanuzzaman and Izlal Hossain, senior program manager of Asia Foundation, said that the program has been effective because of the use of workers associated with microcredit activities as trainers. Due to their pre-existing trust and understanding with the local population, it is possible to spread the knowledge of the training easily.

The event featured a panel discussion on 'Building a Cyber-Resistant Digital Ecosystem for MSMEs in Bangladesh'. Muntasir Tahmid Chowdhury, Managing Director (MD) of Inspira Advisory and Consulting said that cyber crime is now a big threat to large organizations as well as small businesses in remote areas. Awareness, effective security measures and cooperation of related parties are essential to deal with this risk.

Sajeda Foundation Deputy CEO Fazlul Haque said many entrepreneurs are taking digital services without adequate knowledge about passwords, account security and online risks. Apart from this, he mentioned that fraudsters regularly change their tactics and said that cyber security practices should be continued.

Additional Director of Bangladesh Bank also spoke at the event. Khairul Anam and Nadia Subramaniam, Director of Digital Skills at Asia Foundation.
