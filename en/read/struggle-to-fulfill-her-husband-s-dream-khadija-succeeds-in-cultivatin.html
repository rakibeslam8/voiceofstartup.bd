Khadija fights to fulfil her husband's dream, finds success growing foreign fruits

By মীর মাহমুদুল হাসান18 November 2026 at 9:29 pm5 min read

Khadija Akhter (55) was living well with her husband and two sons. Around November 2013, darkness descended on the world. At that time, her husband businessman Nazrul Islam suffered from heart disease. He underwent surgery. A month later, he was diagnosed with a tumor and cancer in his esophagus. At that time husband's treatment cost a huge sum of money. But in the end the husband could not be saved. He died on May 29, 2014.

At the time of Nazrul's death, the elder son was in the second year of graduation and the younger son was in the 11th standard. Both were studying from Rangpur. Khadija's single struggle with two sons who lost their father and a broken family began from that day.

Khadija's house in Shaulmari Sungar village of Kaimari union of Jaldhaka upazila of Nilphamari. After the death of her husband, Khadija was in trouble with her stationery shop in Kaimari Bazar and her family. He had to listen to the bad words of many people while sitting in the shop. However, he continued the shop. Didn't look back. Because, the dream of the husband was to raise the boys.

A recent visit to Khadija's house showed a passion fruit vine at the entrance of the house. Pond on one side, orchard on the other. Khadija said this to herself. In the yard there are various trees including litchi, mango, bay leaf, tamarind. There are flowers hanging in the loft. On one side, a few rectangular concrete houses harbor colorful fish. Rambutan, coffee and cocoa seedlings in the yard. Coffee, rambutan and dragon fruit gardens can be seen from the back door of the house.

starting with coffee beans

Khadija said, 'I like to drink coffee. The elder son said one day, whether it is possible to clear the fallow land next to the house and plant coffee seedlings. I agree. Khadija and her son brought some Arabica coffee seedlings from Kishoreganj upazila after clearing the jungle. Coffee cultivation started in 2018. Then plant rambutan, cocoa, dragon and passion fruit seedlings one by one. Within three years these trees start bearing fruit. At the same time, he started cultivating colorful fish fry by building a concrete rectangular house on one side of the garden.

I like to drink coffee. The elder son said one day, whether it is possible to clear the fallow land next to the house and plant coffee seedlings. I agree. — Khadija Akhter, Women Entrepreneur

Khadija's five bigha land with house, pond, garden. Among them is a house with a pond covering one bigha. The remaining two bighas are coffee and two bighas are various trees including dragon, rambutan, passion fruit, cocoa. He has been marketing coffee for about five years. Coffee is sold at Tk 2,500 to Tk 3,000 per kg. Rambutan is sold at Tk 1,000 to Tk 1,200 per kg. 10 Rambutan plants are yielding. Last year sold 400 kg of rambutan. This year three trees yielded 80 kg, while the remaining seven trees did not yield. This year, colorful fish fry were sold for 5 thousand rupees.

According to Khadija, her monthly income is around 30,000 taka by selling fruits and seedlings excluding all expenses. Now in his garden there are 2500 coffee seedlings, 300 rambutan seedlings and 200 cocoa seedlings. He bought another 75 hundred acres of land with the money earned from the garden.

keeps the husband's word

Relatives once said to bring home at least one son. Khadija did not agree. She said, "Before he died, my husband said that he had many dreams about his sons, but could not do anything for them." If I can teach them education and educate them. I kept my husband's word. This is my biggest achievement.

Elder son md. Akhtaruzzaman holds an MBA from Northern University and is working as a senior executive officer in a private company. Younger son Sefayet Nasrat completed his post graduation from Gazipur Agricultural University in 2025. Besides trying to get a job, he spends time in his mother's garden, selling fruits and seedlings online. Khadija has a Facebook page. Sefayet runs it.

Talked with Khadija's younger son Sefayet while tending to the garden. He said, 'I have completed my studies in Agriculture. I spend my free time in my mother's garden. I am also working in selling fruits and seedlings online. I give whatever help is needed in gardening. Our garden will go further in the days to come—that is my hope.'

Khadija remembers her hard days and says, 'I worked hard. My children, my business—everything is taken care of.'

Acknowledgment and Motivation

Khadija won the best woman agricultural entrepreneur award in the district in 2026. Awarded National Fisheries Week 2022 for producing colorful fish fry.

Chairman of Kaimari Union Parishad (UP). Sadequl Siddique said, Khadija Akhtar is a struggling woman. After the death of her husband, she managed the family, business, education of her sons - all alone. Seeing him, many houses in the village are cultivating local and foreign fruits.

Seeing Khadija's interest, she has been advised several times and materials, seedlings and some equipment have been provided, said Mocha, Deputy Director of the District Agriculture Extension Department. Homayra Mandal. He told Prothom Alo, seeing him, other men and women are also dreaming of becoming entrepreneurs. He said that other entrepreneurs will also be given similar support.
