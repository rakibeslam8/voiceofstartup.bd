Blessings on Halal Earnings: 5 Prophetic Principles for Businessmen

By ইফতেখারুল হক হাসনাইন18 November 2026 at 9:34 pm5 min read

The importance of economic activities in human life is immense. Business plays an important role in making a living, managing a family and meeting the needs of society. Islam encourages people to earn wealth by legitimate means.

At the same time, it has given clear instructions on the method of earning, transparency of transactions and protection of human rights.

In the Holy Qur'an Allah has made trade legal and has declared usury to be haram. (Surah Baqarah, Verse: 275)

Business is not only a means of earning profit, but it is also an act of worship, if it is conducted in an Islamic way. 5 basic principles taught by the Prophet in business are discussed in order to be blessed with halal earnings:

Exaggerating the quality of the product, giving false advertisement or misleading the buyer by false oath is against the ethics of Islam.

1. Maintain honesty and transparency

One of the foundations of business relationships is mutual trust. This trust is established through honesty and transparency. Exaggerating the quality of the product, giving false advertisement or misleading the buyer by false oath is against the ethics of Islam.

The Holy Prophet (PBUH) said, 'Both the buyer and the seller have rights, as long as they are not separated from each other. If they tell the truth and clarify (the condition of the goods), they are blessed in their buying and selling. And if they conceal and lie, the blessing of their buying and selling is removed.' (Sahih Bukhari, Hadith: 2079)

He also warned, "Even if an oath helps in selling goods, it wipes out the blessing." (Sahih Bukhari, Hadith: 2087)

2. Fullness in size and exact quality assurance

Fairness in weights and measures in business transactions is one of the most important guidelines of Islam. The Holy Qur'an strongly condemns underpayment.

Allah says, 'Woe to those who give less in measure. Those who take from people in full when they are measured, and when they are measured or weighed, they give less.' (Surah Mutaffifin, Ayat: 1-3)

It is the responsibility of the trader to give correct information to the buyer not only about the weight but also about the quality of the product. Fraud involves adulteration or concealment of defects in a product.

Once the Holy Prophet (PBUH) passed by a pile of food grains and put his hand into it. When he felt wetness on his finger, he asked the seller why. When the seller told about getting wet in the rain, he said, 'Then why not put the wet part up so that people can see it? He who deceives is not of my party.' (Sahih Muslim, Hadith: 102)

Do not consume each other's wealth unjustly; However, it is different if the business is based on mutual consent.

Surah Nisa, verse: 29

3. Don't misappropriate resources

Islam has given great importance to people's right to wealth. Prohibits acquisition of wealth by fraud, forgery, embezzlement or any other illegal means. Allah says, 'O believers, do not consume each other's wealth unjustly; However, it is different if the business is based on mutual consent.' (Surah Nisa, verse: 29)

Especially, embezzling the wealth of orphans, helpless and weak people, delaying the payment of workers' wages or wrongfully taking possession of the partner's property are serious crimes.

It is said in the Qur'an, "Surely, those who consume the wealth of orphans unjustly, they eat fire in their bellies." (Surah An-Nisa, verse: 10)

4. Abstaining from usury and immoral transactions

Salam expressly forbids usury. There is no scope to legitimize interest based transactions in the name of business and investment. Allah Ta'ala says, 'O you who believe, fear Allah and give up what remains of usury...' (Surah Baqarah, verse 278).

Apart from usury, no profession or business shall be accepted, the basis of which is injustice, exploitation or promotion of obscenity. Islam has given utmost importance to purity and morality in earning.

Unnecessary breach of contract or delay in payment of arrears damages business confidence. So it is necessary to be careful about the terms of the contract at every stage of the business.

5. Keeping agreements and promises

An important element of business relationship is contract and promise. It is the responsibility of each party to comply with the conditions stipulated in the supply of goods, payment of price, completion of work or provision of services.

Allah says, 'And fulfill your promise. Surely the promise will be questioned.' (Surah Isra, verse: 34)

Unnecessary breach of contract or delay in payment of debts damages business confidence. So it is necessary to be careful about the terms of the contract at every stage of the business.

If these guidelines are implemented in business, mutual trust will be established between buyers and sellers and economic exploitation and fraud will be reduced.

At present, these principles of Islam are the most effective solution to prevent various irregularities such as adulteration of products in the market, hoarding, creation of artificial crisis and fraudulent transactions.
