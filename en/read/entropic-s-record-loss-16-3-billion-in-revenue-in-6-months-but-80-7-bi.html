Entropic's record loss: 16.3 billion in revenue in 6 months, but 80.7 billion dollars!

By VOSB Desk22 October 2026 at 8:01 pm2 min read

Anthropic, maker of the popular AI chatbot 'Claude', has faced shocking financial figures for the first half of 2026 (January-June). According to the Wall Street Journal and a leaked IPO draft prospectus, the company had revenue of $16.3 billion at the time, but a net loss of $80.7 billion.

The real reason for such huge losses
While the $80.7 billion loss may seem like a cash crunch for the company, the underlying reasons are different.

Accounting Charges:Of this huge loss, about $72 billion is 'Non-Cash Accounting Charges'. The losses on paper were due to changes in the valuation of the financial instruments that Enthropic had originally issued to raise funds.

Infrastructure and Compute Costs:Their real costs in operational areas are also increasing. In the first six months of 2026, they spent nearly $9 billion on computers and servers, compared to $7.3 billion in the full year of 2025.

BIG LEAP IN INCOMEDespite losses on paper, Entropic's core business has seen incredible growth. Their revenue jumped to $16.3 billion from just $1.1 billion in the same period last year. According to information provided by investors, they earned $16.8 billion in the third quarter (Q3) alone, which is the same as the previous six months in one quarter.Share Market or IPO Plan
By next November (2026) Enthropic is preparing to be listed on the stock market (IPO) as a public limited company. According to reports, their expected market value in the IPO could be around 2 trillion or 2 trillion dollars.
​Future risks and massive cloud contracts
In the coming days, Entropic plans to buy about $750 billion worth of compute capacity with cloud providers to further power its AI technology. These include contracts worth $111 billion with Google, $110 billion with Amazon (AWS), and $31 billion with Microsoft.

But the draft also mentions two big risks: First, companies like Amazon, Google and Microsoft increasingly see Enthropic as a 'competitor' rather than a customer, making it harder to get the servers they need. Second, almost 20 percent of the company's total revenue is coming from the pockets of only two large customers, which is quite risky in the long run.

All in all, Enthropic's skyrocketing revenue growth despite huge losses proves that they are taking a very aggressive approach in this competitive AI sector.
