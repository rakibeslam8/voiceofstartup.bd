Household expenses are increasing, how to save savings

By VOSB Desk7 November 2026 at 8:03 pm7 min read

In 2021, Azhar Hossain (pseudonym) lived on 50 thousand taka per month. Apart from this, some money was also in hand. He used to save that money. But in the last few years, the cost of daily necessities, house rent, children's education and transportation have increased. Now more than 70 thousand rupees are being spent per month to run that household. That means he has to spend at least 20 thousand rupees more than before. The amount of additional expenditure per year is at least 2 lakh 40 thousand rupees.

Azhar Hossain works in a private company. His monthly expenses have increased in the last five years but his salary has not increased at that rate. As a result, now the savings have to be broken. Sometimes do extra work for extra income. In this way, the family is going smoothly, but his physical and mental stress is increasing.

The country's general average inflation has been above 8 percent for more than four years. As a result, Azhar's experience is not limited to his family; Rather, in this reality, many families have to spend more than before. But nobody's income increased like that. According to government reports, the rate of wage growth is lower than inflation. They have to struggle to meet their daily needs.

In this situation, it is important to keep an account of income and expenses, reduce unnecessary expenses and develop the habit of saving according to the ability. However, before deciding the saving strategy, it is necessary to understand how much money is being spent in which sector and where there is an opportunity to reduce the cost.

Which sector is spending more?
The first step in controlling household expenses is to keep an accurate account of income and expenses. For that, a list of possible expenses can be prepared at the beginning of the month. At the end of the month, if you compare the list with the actual expenses, you will understand which sector is spending more money. This will make it easier to plan the expenses for the next month.

Generally household expenses can be divided into several parts. Regular essential expenses include rent, food, electricity, gas, water, transportation and children's education. Personal needs include medical, clothing and other necessary purchases. Eating out, entertainment, sightseeing and immediate shopping are included in variable expenses. On the other hand, money needs to be allocated for savings, emergency fund and debt repayment.

Daily expenses can be recorded in a phone note, notebook or spreadsheet. Small expenses may not seem like much individually, but they can add up to a large sum at the end of the month. So it is important to keep regular account.

Personal savings formula

A widely discussed method is the 50/30/20 rule. Former US Senator Elizabeth Warren and her daughter Amelia Warren Tyagi have outlined this method in their book 'All Your Worth: The Ultimate Lifetime Money Plan'. They suggest allocating 50% of post-tax income to essential expenses, 30% to personal preference and 20% to savings and debt repayment.

Saving rules
A widely discussed approach to personal finance management is the 50/30/20 rule. Former US Senator Elizabeth Warren and her daughter Amelia Warren Tyagi have outlined this method in their book 'All Your Worth: The Ultimate Lifetime Money Plan'.

The rule suggests that 50 percent of post-tax income should be allocated to essential expenses, 30 percent to personal preference and 20 percent to savings and debt repayment.

Another method is the 80/20 formula. In this case, at the beginning of the month, 20 percent of the income is set aside for savings and the remaining 80 percent is tried to run the family. But this ratio can be changed if the income is low. The key is to plan to save as much as you can from the beginning, not just save what's left at the end of the month.

Why is emergency fund necessary?
A sudden medical emergency, job loss, loss of income or any other emergency may arise outside of regular expenses. In a country where more than 80 percent of the economy is in the informal sector, financial uncertainty is high in people's lives. In times of emergency, if there is no savings, one has to borrow or rely on loans. This creates pressure to repay the loan in the future.

For that, importance is given to building an emergency fund in personal finance management. Conventional advice is to set aside three to six months' worth of essential household expenses. It is not mandatory for everyone. Larger funds may be required if income is irregular or if the family has more dependent members.

One also needs to be aware of the cost of the loan. Credit card debt, high-interest personal loans or installment debt can put a big strain on future income. Therefore, before taking a new loan, it is necessary to calculate how much you can afford to pay the installments, or how much the total interest amount is.

In response to the question of what the family can do in this reality of high inflation, Professor Sayema ​​Haque of the Department of Economics of Dhaka University said that the expenditure has increased, but savings should not stop completely. You should save some money regularly. It is possible to reduce expenditure in some sectors which are not essential. Like going out once or twice a month instead of going out every week. Instead of buying Malta for the house, buying local fruit Amra. Cheaper fish can be bought instead of more expensive fish for protein too. There will be no significant variation in nutritional value. Some concessions are possible in terms of quality of life.

Sayema ​​Haque also said that one should be careful in taking loans. It is better not to take loans for consumption. Along with this, the burden of the previous debt should not be dragged on for a long time.

Financial Security Even on Low Income
The life story of a man from Bangalore, India is discussed as an example of saving on low income. According to a report in The Economic Times, the 53-year-old is a proofreader by profession. In 2000, he came to Bangalore from the village with only 5 thousand rupees. He was 27 years old then. He started his career with a monthly salary of 4 thousand 200 rupees. In 2025 it will increase to 63 thousand rupees. He then retired.

According to reports, the amount of money deposited in his bank as a result of long savings is 1 crore 1 lakh rupees. His monthly income from investment is 60 thousand rupees. He has no debt. He lives frugally by avoiding taking out loans and using credit cards and limiting his spending.

Financial freedom does not mean great wealth
For people with limited income, financial freedom does not mean owning huge wealth; Rather, being out of debt, managing emergencies and saving some money for the future are also great achievements.

But discipline of personal savings is not the solution to all problems. Financial security is directly related to income growth, employment stability and cost of living. How much a person can save depends not only on his spending habits; Rather depends on income and family reality.
