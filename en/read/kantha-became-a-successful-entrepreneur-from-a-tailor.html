Kantha became a successful entrepreneur from a tailor

By VOSB Desk4 November 2026 at 11:18 am6 min read

We know about pens made of plastic, steel, wood and bamboo. I also saw pens made of paper. But if that paper pen is thrown away after use, it mixes with the soil and grows green seedlings. Nachima Akhter and her highly educated daughter Rebecca Sultana, resident of Loan Officepara in Jessore city, are successfully doing such wonderful work.

Let's open the story, they invented the process of attaching the seeds to the pen made of eco-friendly paper. The green saplings of the tree are growing from that seed. Seeing such initiative of mother and daughter, many people in the area are now dreaming of becoming entrepreneurs, he said.

Nachima Akhter's family became self-sufficient by selling 'seed pens'. They have been marketing this pen since last January with the aim of contributing to building a plastic-free green world. Everyone throws away the pen after the ink runs out. Then the paper pen gets mixed with the soil. After a few days, seedling germination occurs.

'I started working with eco-friendly products after Corona. We are selling different types of products online including betel leaf plates, bamboo toothbrushes. I contacted Nachima Apa after seeing the advertisement of eco-friendly pen made of paper on Facebook. Seeing him, my daughter and I started making this pen at home on a small scale.
Anwara Khatun

Earlier, Nachima Akhter invented and marketed 'eco-friendly paper pen'. Currently, they are simultaneously making eco-friendly paper pens and paper seeded pens. In the meantime, their invented pen has crossed the borders of the country and touched foreign soil as a sample.

At the retail level, Nachima-Rebeka's seeded paper pen is selling at Tk 15 and paper pen only at Tk 10. However, if they buy more than 100 pens, that is, they sell pens at the wholesale price of 6 and a half to 7 and a half taka. The major buyers of these pens are various non-governmental organizations (NGOs). The name and address of these organizations are printed in the pen. At present, seven artisans make an average of 1,000 pens a day.

Nachima Akhtar said, "There was a joint family. There are many members in the family. My husband is the eldest son of the family. He was working as signalman in BGB. He retired from service in 2000 due to ear problems. He started a fish farm with the money he got during his retirement. But there are losses. We become destitute. Then I started sewing Kantha to run the family. But it earned little money.

The family was not functioning. Later, I tied the paper with the lead of a pen and saw that it was being written well. Then I made 100 pens and took them to school and sold them to the students. He further said, "I used to make pens at home, and my son took them to school and sold them." I left my daughter in Dhaka to study with business money.

The matter of Nachima's eco-friendly pen has come to my attention. As a small and medium entrepreneur, Nachima Akhter has the opportunity to provide loan, training and BC plot on easy terms. Action will be taken if they contact us.
Md. Golam Hafeez, Deputy General Manager, Jessore, Bangladesh Small and Cottage Industries Corporation (BCIC).

Nachima said that she has established an organization named 'Shubh Bibhabbandhav Kalam' in the name of her only son Mir Naeem Akhter Shubhar. Daughter Rebecca Sultana has recently joined her mother's initiative with a bachelor's degree in English literature from a college in Dhaka. The method of attaching seeds to the back of the pen was originally invented by Rebecca.

Rebekah Sultana said in this regard, "I took the pen made by my mother and saw that there is some empty space behind it. The area to be cut. I thought how to use that space.

Then I thought, everyone throws away the pen after use. Pens made of paper mix with soil. I checked whether the small seeds of guava, tomato, saffron, green chillies, vegetables, flowers and fruits can be attached to the empty part of the back of the pen. The idea proved to be successful. So the seeds are being attached to the pen.'

Recently Nachima went to Akter's house and saw seven-eight people making paper pens sitting on the balcony of the house. Some are cutting paper, some are applying glue, some are making pen caps. This is how the pen is made.

At the retail level, Nachima-Rebekah seeded paper pens are being sold at Tk 15 and only paper pens at Tk 10. However, if they buy more than 100 pens, that is, they sell pens at the wholesale price of 6 and a half to 7 and a half taka. The major buyers of these pens are various non-governmental organizations (NGOs).

Nachima's son Mir Naeem Akhtar is doing the pen marketing. He said, 'Our pens are being sent to 64 districts of the country. However, our pen is going more in various private organizations. Among these NGOs, Nari Paksha, Jago Nari Development Organization, Nari Maitri, environmental organization Bela, CCDB, Bandhu Foundation and BIGD are notable.

Many people buy pens at wholesale price from Nachima and sell them at retail. Anwara Khatun of Mylapota area of ​​Khulna used to sell this pen at various fairs or festivals. He has been making this pen himself for three months.

Anwara Khatun told Prothom Alo, 'I started working with eco-friendly products after Corona. We are selling different types of products online including betel leaf plates, bamboo toothbrushes. I contacted Nachima Apa after seeing the advertisement of eco-friendly pen made of paper on Facebook. Seeing him, my daughter and I started making this pen at home on a small scale.

Rebecca Sultana said, "I have a bachelor's degree in English literature but I have no desire to work." My goal is to grow my mother's business. Pens have already been experimentally made from jute. The aim is to create more eco-friendly products, which will employ at least 100 women. But due to lack of capital we are not able to do that. We will be able to advance this initiative if we get government grants.

When asked about eco-friendly pens, Deputy General Manager of Bangladesh Small and Cottage Industries Corporation (BCIC) Jessore said.
