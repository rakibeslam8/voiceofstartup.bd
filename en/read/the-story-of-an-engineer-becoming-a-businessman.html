The story of an engineer becoming a businessman

By VOSB Desk9 November 2026 at 9:24 pm10 min read

He passed from Bangladesh University of Engineering (BUET) in 1977. The subject was Civil Engineering. It was relatively easy to get jobs for engineers then. There were even opportunities to work outside the country. The newly passed engineer. Abdul Awal did both. Worked, in a word left the job. He went abroad and came back again. Those events are no less surprising. Finally settled in housing business. The Structural Engineers Limited founded by him is now a big name in the housing sector. He told that story while sitting in Panthapath's office.

Desire was to teach in Buete. But he did not get that opportunity because he did not get the first class. First job was as an assistant engineer in construction company Rana Construction. The first project of his career was the construction of BUET's auditorium complex. Professor Ali Murtaza was in charge of project supervision. At work, he asked why he was not joining BUET. When he heard that he did not get first class, he was quite surprised.

Zahurul Islam's BDC was then associated with construction business in Abu Dhabi. Although he was appointed there, he did not go to Abu Dhabi. Then the job was very attractive to many. Assistant Engineer in Power Development Board. And the posting is at Riazuddin Bazar office in Chittagong. Engineer Md. Abdul Awal told about that incident himself. He said, 'The place is a commercial area. So in any case it was a very tempting posting. I did not explain why it is attractive. I went there on November 9, 1977. The next day it was his turn to take responsibility. But the person from whom I will take over was not present. Then I sat on the opposite side of the room and thought for an hour, what should be done. I think this job is not for me. That's when I decided not to do this job. I returned to Dhaka that day.

The first best decision in life
It's time to return to Dhaka and look for a job again. Interest in consulting firm jobs. There is also a shock. At that time, there was a consulting organization named Engineering Sangsad. Alimullah Khan. There, engineer Harunur Rashid said, how can you get a job in a consulting company after getting a second class. The insistence was suppressed by these words. In the end, he got a job in a consulting firm. He had two jobs at the same time. Among them, the office of Engineering Consultants and Associates (ECA) was at Dhanmondi Road No. 2, next to City College, and the office of Bangladesh Consultants Limited (BCL) was at Dilkusha. He was then living in Elephant Road.

But he joined the far office. Find out who has good projects, where there are more opportunities to work, with whom to work. Even today he feels that joining BCL in 1978 was the best decision of his life.
The founder of BCL was M Muhibur Majid. He passed engineering from Shivpur in 1935. Another company founded by him is The Engineers Limited. Both institutes dealt with civil engineering, but M Muhibur Majid was an electrical engineer. Within 15 days of joining there, he got one of the biggest lessons of his life.

Let's hear from his mouth, 'Drydock Limited in Chittagong repairs sea going ships. The drydock's four-and-a-half-foot-wide concrete walls were bursting with water. Then BCL and British consultancy firm RPT visited the site and gave a report. It was decided to send me to supervise the repair work of that crack. The day before I left, I fell in front of M. Muhibur Masjid. He asked if I knew why the wall was broken. I didn't know then. Then he brought the report. He told me to read the whole thing. And he said, there is no end to knowing that it is right. But whatever he learns, he will learn as he learns, and whatever he knows, he will know completely. Open your ears to hear what you see, open your eyes to see what you see.

He also resigned from this BCL in one word. There is an interesting story behind it. Md. Abdul Awal was working on several projects then. He used to go to the project area in the morning, from there he would go to the head office at some point. He used to sign the attendance book when he went to the head office. Suddenly, one day, he saw a small 'A' written in pencil next to his name, meaning absent. He signed it there. The next day, 'A' next to the name again, this time written in pen ink. He signed there again. Next day 'A' is written in red ink. He could not accept that there will be red ink stains in his working life. He immediately gave his resignation letter. Although he resigned, he was not allowed to leave the service.

How he came back from Iraq
Engineer Md. Abdul Awal worked at BCL for four years. After that he decided to go to Iraq for a Japanese company. The time was October 1981. He thought he would get a chance to do something new outside the boundaries of the country. But the idea was wrong. They were taken to show how many engineers there are. And in the beginning there was a quarrel about some technical issues. A couple of Japanese wanted to blame their mistakes on the Bangladeshi engineers.

He returned to the country after only six months. Mahbubul Haque, the then managing director of BSL, took a one-and-a-half-year unpaid leave without submitting his resignation letter before going to Iraq. With additional two months salary. However, before going to Iraq, a businessman had done the structural design of a gentleman's house. That gentleman said to quit his job to do business. Returning in 1982, he went to that gentleman's house and heard the story of becoming a businessman. Then he decided not to work, but to do business.

The story of becoming a businessman
Established his own company The Structural Engineers Limited on December 6, 1983. It was a construction contracting company. However, he did not feel very comfortable even after doing the contracting business for 14 years. Many things were difficult to adjust to. So the thought of doing something alternative was in mind. Then he entered the housing sector. There is a story behind this.

In 1988 there were floods across the country. Then the price of one bag of cement increased from 105 taka to 260-265 taka. The price of the rod also doubles. As a result, the business runs out of capital to complete the work at hand. Then he decided to go to Singapore, do business or work there. He was in Singapore for 35 days in two rounds. But suddenly it was not possible to start business there.

Describing his experience in Singapore, he said, 'In 1991, I stayed in a hotel in Singapore. The owner of the hotel used to sit there in the evening. Usually no one stays in a hotel for a long time here. Seeing that I have been there for more than seven days, he asked to know my identity. After hearing everything, he sent me to a businessman's office. I went and saw a Bangladeshi passport in the hand of a person on a table. At one stage, the owner of the company said that if he brings workers from Bangladesh, he will provide jobs, but for that he will have to pay 3 thousand US dollars per person. After saying that it is not possible for me, the hotel owner suggested to buy a big television and take it to the country. At that time, the television picture tube occupied a large area, and the space around it was empty. There is a profit if you fill various products and bring them to the country and sell them. Many people did the work then. I said it's not my job either. Then the hotel owner suggested to get a job.

After returning to the country of service, he went to Singapore again in 1992. Abdul Awal. This time he went to work. Got a job in a construction company. Went to Singapore on Wednesday, got job on Friday, scheduled to join work on Monday. In other words, there was two days to think. In the end he did not even join the work. He decided to stay in the country. He said, returning to the country of service was another best decision in life.

How in Housing
After coming to the country, at one stage he changed his business and entered the housing sector. For this, however, he recalled the great role of recently deceased architect Mobaswer Hossain. That was in 1995. It was he who called one day and asked if he would do real estate business. Then the story is like this, 'I said who will give me land. Then he said, there is a place on Green Road.

The owner of the land lived in Sweden, then came to the country. Mobashwa's brother spoke and told me the conditions. It was very simple. 6 and a half khata land, and in total you have to pay 45 lakh taka. 30 lakh cash in three installments. Another flat is to be handed over in unfinished condition for Tk 15 lakh. That's the beginning. But he didn't have much money. Even though it was difficult, I finished the work. It increased courage. I thought I would not do the contracting business anymore, I would stay in real estate.

The second project was in Pallavi, I bought that land myself. But there also had to suffer. Because I could not sell the flat. Trusting customers came only after the entire structure was erected. The next project was in Dhanmondi area, near Shankar. Had to suffer there too.

However, we were stopped by a project on the next Elephant Road. But there is a good story here. Negotiations with the land owner about the project were not successful due to conflict of demand. Once it was cancelled. But one morning after the Fajr prayer, the husband of the land owner called the gentleman and asked for the address of the house. He came home and told me to start the work by giving him five lakh taka. At the beginning of the work of that project, some additional money came in when the flats of the company were sold. With this money, Pallavi and Dhanmondi projects were quickly completed and handed over to the buyers. Then there was no looking back.

Finally, the engineer. Abdul Awal said, 'A few days after joining Rana Construction, Director Engineer Yusuf Hossain asked me - what is our identity, are we engineers or businessmen? He gave the answer - we are engineers first, then businessmen. We will do business with our knowledge, with intelligence, with talent, not by stealing; Which I still hold to this day and apply at all levels of SEL.'
