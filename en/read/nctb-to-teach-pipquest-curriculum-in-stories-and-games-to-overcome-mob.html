NCTB to teach 'PipQuest' curriculum in stories and games to overcome mobile games addiction

By VOSB Desk27 November 2026 at 11:10 pm3 min read

A school teacher's two-month research is developing a story-based educational app that runs without the Internet, with educational rewards at learning points.

At present, the biggest concern of parents of students aged 7 to 14 years is the excessive addiction of children to mobile phones and video games. School teacher and entrepreneur Khandaker Mamunur Rashid has started working on a practical solution to these two problems - children's reluctance to learn by rote learning of textbooks on the one hand, and addiction to unnecessary games on smartphones - on the other hand. He is developing story-based practice app 'PipQuest' for third to sixth grade students following National Curriculum and Textbook Board (NCTB) curriculum.

How PipQuest's Journey Began
As a regular school teacher by profession, Khandkar Mamunur Rashid has seen first hand in and around the classroom how young students are becoming more attracted to mobile games than studies. He has been doing intensive research for the past two months with the idea of ​​getting children out of this problem.

His main goal is to create a positive educational medium where children can learn to read their textbooks in the guise of fun and play, away from harmful mobile games and unnecessary screen-addiction. This integrated initiative to provide offline-supported story-based exercises and education-gifts in exchange for points for primary and lower-secondary level students is completely new and exceptional in the context of Bangladesh.

Salient Features of PipQuest
The PipQuest platform is designed with the needs of parents and students in mind. Its main features include:

  • Story-based and joyful learning: NCTB curriculum lessons for classes III to VI are arranged through interesting stories and games (Gamified learning) instead of monotonous presentation, so that students can learn with joy.
  • OFFLINE FUNCTIONALITY WITHOUT INTERNET: Its lessons, audio and learning progress (Progress tracking) works seamlessly without internet connection after downloading apps and educational materials through online platforms. As a result, children are not at risk of being constantly connected to the Internet or online distractions.
  • Earning Points & Educational Rewards: Students will earn points for passing each educational game or step. There are book or education-related gifts for them when they meet certain point levels, which will create a spontaneous interest in reading among children.
  • Role-based Access: The app has separate role-based access for students, teachers or examiners and parents. Through this, parents and teachers can easily monitor the student's learning progress.
  • Web Content Management System (Web CMS): The platform includes a modern Web Content Management System (CMS) to manage and regularly update curriculum-based stories, audio and practice content.

Why is this a reliable solution for parents?

The age of 7 to 14 years is the most important period for children's learning habits and mental development. At this age, learning through curiosity and joy rather than being forced into books lasts longer. With the combination of audio and story in PipQuest, children can easily understand difficult topics, and parents can rest assured because of the convenience of using it without internet.
Also, earning points for completing each step of learning and receiving small educational gifts based on those points will create positive competition and confidence in children.

Future Goals
Khandkar Mamunur Rashid hopes that through PipQuest, third to sixth class students from every corner of the country will get rid of their addiction to mobile games and return to the world of joyful learning. This initiative is expected to open a new horizon of digital innovation in primary and secondary education in Bangladesh with the combined participation of students, teachers and parents.
