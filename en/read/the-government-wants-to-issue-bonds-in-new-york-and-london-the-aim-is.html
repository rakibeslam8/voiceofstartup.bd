The government wants to issue bonds in New York and London, the aim is to reduce bank debt

By বিশেষ প্রতিনিধি20 November 2026 at 0:43 pm6 min read

The government wants to reduce the sole dependence on the banking sector for financing large and long-term investments. For this, initiatives have been taken to strengthen the capital market and bond market.

At the same time, in order to keep the risk of loan repayment to a minimum level, the amount of borrowing is being increased through long-term bonds and Islamic Shariah-based investment product 'Sukuk' instead of short-term treasury bills.

Finance and Planning Minister Amir Khosru Mahmud Chowdhury informed this information in a press briefing organized on the occasion of 'International Debt Management Conference Bangladesh 2026' in Cox's Bazar today. Earlier he inaugurated the conference.

In the press briefing, Prime Minister's Special Assistant Tanveer Gani said that the private sector has mainly taken loans from banks for so long. They did not go to the capital market and did not issue bonds. As a result, credit pressure has been created on the banks. Because of this, many private sector organizations have suffered while taking loans at high interest rates. Alternative sources of financing including bonds are being opened up not only to the government but also to the private sector.

Tanveer Gani said that the government wants to issue bonds in the international market within the next two to three months. These bonds can be traded in New York or London markets. However, the interest rate of the bond and the process by which it will be released have not yet been decided. He said that the work is going on.

This two-day long international conference has been organized for the first time in the country by the joint initiative of the Finance Department of the Ministry of Finance and the Asian Development Bank (ADB).

Nazar Sarkar on global bond market

In the press briefing, among the three main goals of the conference, special importance has been given to the expansion of bond and capital markets. According to the government's plan, initiatives will be taken to release US dollar Eurobonds, Sukuk, Japan's 'Samurai Bond' and China's 'Panda Bond' in order to enter the international capital market at the appropriate time.

Steps are also being taken to make the country's bond market accessible to foreign investors. As part of this, the inclusion of local bonds of Bangladesh in JP Morgan's 'GBI-EM Edge' index will be utilised. Besides, there are plans to connect with international clearing houses 'Euroclear' and 'Clearstream' to facilitate the settlement of transactions for foreign investors.

Apart from this, the finance minister announced plans to increase the scope of environment-friendly 'green bonds' and marine economy-based 'blue bonds' in addition to sukuk to strengthen the investor base at the local and international level. He also said that initiatives have been taken to attract pension funds, insurance companies and foreign investors to strengthen the local bond market.

Debt interest rates are down, long-term bonds are on the rise

Finance Minister said, 'It is not how much loan was taken; How well the loan is utilized is the real thing.

According to the information given in the press briefing, the macroeconomic stability has returned and the borrowing cost of the government has decreased as Bangladesh Bank has stopped covering the budget deficit by printing money. The interest rate on the 91-day Treasury bill was around 12 percent in June 2025. In June 2026, it fell below 10 percent. Currently this interest rate is between 8 to 9 percent. Now the government is mainly borrowing through long-term bonds and sukuk.

The finance minister also mentioned in the briefing that the debt report (debt report) has been published and the primary dealer (PD) system has been modernized to bring transparency in bond and debt management. Apart from this, the 'Annual Borrowing Plan' and the new Medium Term Borrowing Strategy are being published for the first time so that market participants can know the government's borrowing plans in advance.

Comparative picture of credit situation

The briefing also presented a picture of the debt situation before the change of government on August 5, 2024, i.e. till June 30, 2024. It is said that on June 30, 2024, the amount of government debt was 18 lakh 89 thousand crores, which is 36 percent of GDP. In the previous two years (June 30, 2022 to June 30, 2024), government debt increased by Tk 545,000 crore. At the same time, Bangladesh Bank's loan to the government increased from Tk 730 crore to Tk 56 thousand 750 crore. Foreign exchange reserves also fell to $20.5 billion. As a result inflation rose to around 10 percent. However, the amount of borrowing by the government from the central bank has decreased in the later period. On June 30, 2026, it came down to Tk 482 crore.

It was informed in the briefing that the current government's actions have changed that situation. By June 30, 2026, Bangladesh Bank's loan to the government has come down to Tk 482 crore. And according to the IMF's accounting methodology (BPM6), foreign exchange reserves have exceeded $30 billion.

Debt repayment pressure ahead, reforms underway

The finance minister said that the time has come to repay the loans taken in the past. In FY 2025-26, principal and interest payments on foreign debt are being spent on $4.5 billion. In the fiscal year 2029-30, this expenditure will rise to a maximum of 5.5 billion dollars.

The finance minister said that increasing revenue is the biggest reform for sustainable management of debt. For that, expansion of tax net, reduction of unreasonable tax exemptions and digitization of VAT and income tax system are being done.

Besides, restructuring of the capital of government banks, bringing back order in the banking sector, formation of integrated 'Debt Management Office' (DMO) and formulation of 'Public Debt Act' through timely amendments is going on.

The finance minister expressed hope that realistic recommendations will be obtained from the conference, keeping in mind the goal of building a trillion dollar economy by 2034 and transitioning from the list of naturally least developed countries (LDC) in 2029.

World Bank, International Monetary Fund (IMF), ADB and experts from USA, UK, Malaysia, Indonesia, Pakistan and Sri Lanka are participating in the conference.
