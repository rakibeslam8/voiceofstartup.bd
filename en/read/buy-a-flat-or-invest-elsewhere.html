Buy a flat or invest elsewhere

By VOSB Desk7 November 2026 at 8:31 pm6 min read

Jobber AKM Azhar has booked a flat in Bachila of the capital. He does not have a very high paying job. He is the only earner in the family. His family with wife and two children.

Azhar took a loan of Tk 20 lakh from a private bank for a period of 5 years to buy a flat. He still could not get up to his flat. But every month I have to pay 45 thousand 250 rupees. As the tenure of the loan is shorter, the amount of installments is also higher. As a result, a large part of his monthly income is going towards loan installments. Along with this is the current house rent and other household expenses.

For many like Azhar, owning a flat is one of the biggest goals in life. It also has reality. But from an investment point of view, the matter is a little different. Buying a flat for own residence and buying a flat as an investment are not the same thing. From an investment point of view, it should be seen whether buying a flat is the best use of money.

Let's say you live in a house in the capital with a monthly rent of 40,000 taka. You have no debt and have Rs 50 lakh as long term savings. Now if you want, you can take the initiative to buy a flat with this money, the remaining money will be taken as a bank loan.

If you buy a flat, you don't have to pay 40,000 rupees per month, it's not right to see the matter like this; Rather, it should be seen how much income could have been earned if the money spent on buying a flat was invested in some other sector. Also, loan interest, installments, maintenance costs, taxes and other expenses should also be factored into the calculation. This is known as opportunity cost in economics. That is, because of spending money in one place, it is necessary to take into account the fact that you cannot make a profit by investing money in another place or that you have to give up.

Let's say you need one crore taka to buy a flat in Dhaka. Monthly expenses including flat rent and garage rent are 40,000 taka. Annual expenditure will be 4 lakh 80 thousand taka. Looking at this calculation, many people may think that it is better to buy a flat than stay in a rented house for a few years.

But instead of buying a flat with one crore taka, what can happen if that money is invested in some other sector, we have to see. If an average return or profit of 8 percent is obtained, the income can be from 1 crore to about 8 lakh rupees per year. That is, an average of about 66 thousand 667 taka per month. However, it may be less than 8 percent.

In other words, in buying a flat, you are saving Rs 4 lakh 80 thousand per annum on rent, while at the same time losing Rs 1 crore of income that could have been earned by investing elsewhere. Therefore, to understand the real financial benefits of flats, the rental savings and the potential return on investment have to be compared.

One more thing is important here—how much the flat prices will increase in the future. If the price of the flat increases in the long term, the value of the owner's assets will also increase, but that is not guaranteed. Its price depends a lot on the area of ​​the flat, age of the building, construction quality, communication system, urban development and future demand.

On the other hand, if you own a rental property, your capital is usable. If necessary, the type of investment can be changed. However, the value of money decreases with time, that should also be taken into consideration. On the other hand, in the case of flats, a large amount of money is stuck in one place, it is not easy to cash it quickly in an emergency.

If you buy a flat with a loan, the calculation becomes more complicated. Let's say you have Tk 50 lakh and take a loan of Tk 50 lakh to buy a flat of Tk 1 crore. Then 50 lakhs will go to the flat at once. Besides, the loan installments have to be paid every month. The interest on this loan will greatly increase the actual cost of the flat.

In this case, it is necessary to compare the total interest of the loan with the possible increase in the price of the flat. It is not only the increase in the price of flats that will make the investment profitable. Registration at the time of purchase, taxes, fees, other expenses and maintenance costs at the time of ownership are also part of the total investment cost.

However, not all calculations can be done in monetary terms. The security of living in your own home, the freedom to create the environment you want, the hassle-free relationship with your landlord, and a permanent address—these are important to many. Many people think that owning a home for retirement is part of financial security. Overall wealth increases.

What to consider
Whether to rent, or to pay in installments—this is not the right comparison to make when deciding to buy a flat for your own living. From an investment point of view, the factors to be taken into consideration are the potential return on investment in flats, the potential return on alternative investments, the cost of borrowing and the ability to bear the consequences of capital being stuck.

The most important thing is to have enough emergency savings in hand even after buying a flat. A family's financial risk may increase if the entire savings is poured into the flat and the loan is taken.

Flats are ultimately not just investments; It is also an asset. Therefore, besides financial returns, one's own income, debt repayment capacity, future plans and risk-taking capacity should be taken into consideration in the decision-making process.
