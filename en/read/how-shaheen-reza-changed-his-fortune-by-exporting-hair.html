How Shaheen Reza changed his fortune by exporting hair

By VOSB Desk6 November 2026 at 11:37 am7 min read

The son of the remote village of Jhenaidah has changed his fortune by selling the discarded hair of people's heads. Shaheen Reza In his youth, his dream was to study in Dhaka University, then do business. Although his first dream was not fulfilled, his dream of becoming a businessman was fulfilled by his own efforts and hard work. As a businessman, he also received a national award for his contribution to the export sector. But he started the business venture with only 345 rupees capital and two workers. Now more than thousand people are employed in his company.

Shaheen's company Hair Style Factory received the Silver Award in National Exports for the financial year 2020-21 for contributing to the export trade through the export of discarded head hair. Recently, the Ministry of Commerce presented the award to the company in a ceremony in the capital Dhaka. Last Thursday, the founder of the company spoke at the hair style office in Uttara of the capital. With Shaheen Razor. The story of his becoming an entrepreneur comes out in words.

Shaheen Reza said, there is a common saying about hair, hair is half the beauty. So hair has always been in demand as a part of beauty. Hair is now part of fashion too. Many stars, actors or models around the world use wigs. Because of this, the demand for wigs has also increased among common people. Due to the demand, naturally, many institutions of this sector have been established in the country. Several companies in the country are now involved in the wig business. There are some companies among them, who collect hair and sell it to local and foreign buyers after primary processing. The Shaheen Razor company is like that.

Md. Shaheen Reza's house is in Baidanga village of Saganna Union of Jhenaidah Sadar Upazila. Father is a farmer, mother is a housewife. He got interested in business while studying commerce in secondary school. He moved to Dhaka in 2003 after finishing secondary and higher secondary in Jhenaidah. I wanted to get admission in Dhaka University. But in the end his dream was not fulfilled. Later, on the advice of his elder brother, he was admitted to a local degree college in Jhenaidah.

Meanwhile Shaheen was admitted in Jhenaidah but lived in Dhaka. At this time, he joined various retail businesses along with tuition to support his expenses. But could not benefit much. In 2004, Shaheen lived in a mess in Mohammadpur, Dhaka. Being a village boy, he used to brush his teeth with coal. One morning, thinking of coal, he put his hand in a small polythene and saw a bag full of hair. Then suddenly he thought, hair has a business. Tried to find out about it on the internet and from acquaintances for a few days. Then with only 345 rupees and two workers, he went into the business of collecting discarded hair.

In the beginning, Shaheen used to look for hair in various thrift stores in the capital. While doing these works, he met more than a thousand hawkers from different districts including Dhaka. However, at that time, the number of wig businesses was low, so the income was low. There was no contact with foreign buyers.

Speed in sales to overseas buyers
In 2007, Shaheen got in touch with two Chinese buyers through a known hawker. Since then he used to collect hair for various foreign buyers. But all transactions were done informally. In 2010, he received a purchase order from a large wig factory in the EPZ (Export Processing Zone) of the country. However, the institution stipulates the formal transaction of money. Because of this, Shaheen had to do various tasks one by one, including business registration, opening bank accounts, opening loan documents.

If there is a good income in this way, start planning to expand the business. After initial collection of fine hair, it is processed (separated from tangles and cleaned) and sold for higher prices. For this, around 2012, he rented a house in Nawabganj upazila of Dinajpur and started processing.

Currently, there are 69 permanent employees in Shaheen's hair style factory. Apart from this, about one and a half thousand workers work part-time in his company in Dhaka and Dinajpur. In FY 2020-21, Hairstyle exported hair worth around US$ 8.3 lakh (8.29 million). For this, the company received the National Export Trophy (Silver) from the Ministry of Commerce. In the last financial year 2021-22, it exported hair worth 1 crore 24 lakh dollars. However, due to the global economic crisis this year, exports have decreased a lot, said Shaheen Reza.

According to Export Promotion Bureau (EPB) data, wigs were exported worth USD 12.3 million in the outgoing fiscal year 2022-23. In the previous fiscal year 2021-22, the export amount was 10.59 million dollars. Accordingly, exports have increased by 16 percent in one year. Traders in this sector say that there is a significant amount of trade in the export of wigs from the country through various informal channels. Due to this, those who transact through formal channels, get less orders.

Overcome the barrier
Shaheen Reza has faced various ups and downs after starting the business. He said, from 2013 to 2017, export demand was low. It puts a strain on the capital. In addition, loans have to be made. The wig business is very complicated. If I had known earlier, I would not have joined this business. Along with the business crisis, Shaheen Reza has also faced various external obstacles while doing hair business. He said, around 2007, people did not know much about this business. So often hawkers were stopped at different places. Local people were also often suspicious of hawkers. Now with increasing awareness these barriers have reduced.

Shaheen also gives some interesting information about hair collection. For example, they get more and better quality hair from big cities including the capital Dhaka. Shaheen said that the quality of hair is better because urban citizens use more hair tools. Apart from this, people in the river area tend to have more hair fall. Due to this, they collect more hair from southern districts.

Hawkers usually collect discarded hair from homes, beauty parlors and garbage dumps. One or more hands turn from them and the hair comes into the hands of traders like Shaheen. Price per kg of hair varies from Tk 200 to Tk 6 thousand depending on the quality. And after processing, these hairs are sold for 15 to 300 dollars per kg, said Shaheen Reza.

Hair Style Factory is currently making wigs from discarded human hair on a small scale. But want to make it bigger. He has already constructed a six-storey building in Ashulia. He also brought some wig making equipment from China to work there. Now this entrepreneur is looking for domestic and foreign investment to start work on a large scale.

Besides running the business, he has also built an organization called Rupali Foundation. From this foundation, entrepreneur Shaheen is associated with the care of village children and various kinds of support for students.
