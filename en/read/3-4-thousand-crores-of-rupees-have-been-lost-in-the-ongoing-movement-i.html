3-4 thousand crores of rupees have been lost in the ongoing movement in the garment sector

By নিজস্ব প্রতিবেদক24 November 2026 at 5:57 pm2 min read

Leaders of Bangladesh Garment Buying House Association (BGBA) fear that there has been a loss of 3 to 4 thousand crore rupees in the ongoing wage movement of ready-made garment workers.

BGBA leaders said that if the situation does not improve quickly, the amount of damage will increase every day. The negative impact of this loss will not be limited to the garment industry; Rather, it will fall on the overall economy of the country including transportation, banking, logistics, and other related sectors.

The leaders of the organization said this at a press conference at the BGBA office in Uttara of the capital today. BGBA president Md. read the written statement. Abdul Hamid. Mohammad Zakir Hossain, Senior Vice President of the organization, Roman Mia, Emdadul Haque Miyaji and others were also present at this time.

BGBA president in a written statement. Abdul Hamid said, "In the context of rising cost of living and inflation, the demand for wage increase is very logical. The work of the minimum wage board is going on in this regard. We are hopeful that the concerned parties will be able to arrive at a timely wage structure, which will be fair and equitable for the workers on the one hand, and affordable for the factories on the other hand.”

The BGBA president said that the ongoing unrest is not only disrupting the production of new products, but also delaying the scheduled shipment of manufactured products. If the next season's product is not delivered on time, it may be necessary to send the product by air at an additional cost. Discounting pressure may come from buyers. The most worrying thing is that Bangladesh's business credibility and reliability in the international market is at risk.

Abdul Hamid claimed that the ongoing wage movement is affecting the purchase order. He said that due to the ongoing situation, many international buyers are becoming cautious and hesitant to place new purchase orders from Bangladesh. In some cases, the amount of purchase orders has also decreased. If this trend continues, direct manufacturing factories will suffer. Many factories will not be able to operate at their full production capacity. Some factories may be forced to stop production operations. As a result, not only the capacity and export earnings of the industry will suffer, but the livelihood and financial security of numerous workers and their families will also be threatened.
