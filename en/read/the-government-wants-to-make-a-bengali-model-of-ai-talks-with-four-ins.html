The government wants to make a Bengali model of AI, talks with four institutions

By VOSB Desk9 November 2026 at 9:01 am7 min read

The government is planning to create its own model of artificial intelligence (AI) in Bengali. For this, the country wants to build a strong computer and server infrastructure. The aim is to create and use AI while keeping the important information of the country within the country. Negotiations are underway with the world's top four technology companies to implement the initiative. There are plans to get technical support and necessary parts from them. Apart from government institutions, universities and indigenous technology entrepreneurs will also get the opportunity to use this infrastructure.

As planned by the government, the country's core AI infrastructure will be centered around the National Data Center at Kaliakore in Gazipur. There will be eight regional centers across the country. The National Data Center is currently managed by Bangladesh Data Center Company Limited (BDCCL). Information Technology Ministry sources say that initially 10,000 square feet of space has been allotted in Gazipur.

There are plans to set up eight regional AI computing nodes in eight divisions of the country outside Kaliakor. Officials of the ministry say, as a result, government institutions and universities at the departmental level will get an opportunity to use AI technology locally.

When asked about this, Prime Minister's Post, Telecommunication and Information Technology Advisor Rehan Asif Asad told Prothom Alo, "The main point of sovereign AI is to establish their own control over the information, data and citizen services of the citizens of the country. At the same time, the benefits of AI technology should not be limited to any particular class of society; Rather, everyone from the common farmer to the children of rickshaw pullers gets it, to ensure that.

Rehan Asif Asad said that along with the development of Bengali Large Language Model (LLM) in the country, an effective environment for research and development will be created by connecting the universities. Once the national infrastructure is built, its computing capabilities will be made available to the private sector and startups in the country.

AI Model in Bangla

The government's plan calls for making the existing AI models like Gema, Lama and Kuen suitable for Bengali language. For this, the models will be further trained with Bengali texts and information. In tech parlance this process is called fine tuning. There are also plans to use AI in Bengali speech-to-text creation, translation and educational support. Training and deployment of the model—both tasks—are targeted at in-country infrastructure.

As per the plan, in the first 12 months of the initiative, preparations for adding AI computers, training of staff, installation of small-scale AI systems at the enterprise level and technical survey of the infrastructure of Kaliakor are scheduled. A phase of 12 to 24 months aims to launch the departmental center and train the first AI model. Then there are plans to scale up the capacity of the national center over a period of 24 to 36 months or more and expand access to small and medium enterprises and startups.

why own AI

AI services such as ChatGPT and Gemini are now supporting research and professional work as well. They can be used to find information from different sources on a topic, create detailed reports, compare statements from multiple research papers, and analyze long documents. These AIs are also being used in software development. You don't have to spend a lot of money to do many of these things. So why Bangladesh needs to create a separate AI infrastructure to have these services.

Ministry officials say that running AI technology requires more than just software; Requires powerful computers, processors, servers and large amount of data storage. This computing capability is also needed to train AI on a subject or to answer questions from many people at once. The government wants to build that infrastructure in the country itself.

However, experts say the capabilities of sovereign AI will depend on the quality of the AI ​​model, the required data, training and technical expertise. And having a server in the country will not ensure the security of information. For this, there should be effective security measures and specific rules for data usage.

Fauzia Afroz, head of Bangladesh Tech Global Institute, told Prothom Alo that it is not enough to build one's own infrastructure; It must ensure security, civil rights, legal protection and accountability. He said there is a lack of accountability and structural weakness in data breach cases in Bangladesh. Here policy is considered later with emphasis on infrastructure and technological processes. As a result, it becomes difficult to take effective action in case of data breach. According to him, effective policies and proper understanding with international technology organizations are necessary in addition to data protection.

Government wants to use TESIS

Another goal of the sovereign AI initiative is to incorporate some equipment domestically rather than importing all ready-made equipment from abroad. For this, it is proposed to use the existing factory of the State-owned Telephone Industry Corporation (TESIS) at Tongi in Gazipur.

As per the plan, small-scale AI computers will be added initially. Later the capability to add high capacity workstations, servers and full server racks will be built. But not all parts will be made in Bangladesh. In particular, some important technologies including high-capacity processors and memory should be brought from abroad.

Initially, the plan is to start operations by importing complete equipment. At the same time local factories will be prepared. In the next step, various parts will be brought from abroad and assembled and tested in the country. The next step will be to add various parts of the server, verify the quality of the equipment and build the repair capability.

also have questions

The announcement of creating a domestic alternative to foreign technical services was also made during the Awami League government. In July 2021, the then Minister of State for Information and Communication Technology, Junaid Ahmed Palak announced the creation of Facebook's alternative 'Yogayoga'. He also talked about Zoom's alternative 'Meeting' and WhatsApp's alternative 'Alapan'. Again, in July 2024, AI-based 'G-Brain' platform was inaugurated, it is said to be taken to the national data center and expanded to various sectors including education, health.

However, there are questions about how much implementation has been done after the announcement and how much the services of the inauguration have been useful. Experts say that new initiatives need to learn from past experiences to clarify costs, regular maintenance and performance checks.

When asked, Professor BM Moinul Hossain of Dhaka University's Institute of Information Technology told Prothom Alo that it is not enough to create alternatives to foreign technology services, it is also necessary to ensure that they meet the needs of users. Apart from that, it should also be clear whether the domestic technology will be used for any additional benefit, apart from the technology services widely used worldwide. Such initiatives should not be limited to creating or launching new platforms; Quality of service, security, user interest and long-term management capability should be taken into consideration. Otherwise, these initiatives made at a huge cost may become ineffective due to lack of users.
