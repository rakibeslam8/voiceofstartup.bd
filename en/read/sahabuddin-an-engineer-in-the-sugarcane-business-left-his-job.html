Sahabuddin, an engineer in the sugarcane business, left his job

By VOSB Desk9 November 2026 at 8:38 am5 min read

He has cultivated sugarcane on 100 bighas of land in that area through contract farming. Due to his initiative, Amrit variety sugarcane is becoming popular in the area.

Engineer Sahabuddin's father Ketabuddin used to make jaggery from sugarcane and sell that jaggery in the local market. After completing his education from Chittagong University of Engineering and Technology or Chuet, the engineer son took over the father's business. He made four new types out of common molasses. Chota patali, jhola gur, powder gur and chocolate shaped gur - these four types of gur are made in the business. This season, Sahabuddin aims to sell 100 tonnes of jaggery. Already sold 70 tons. Sugarcane season lasts till April.

Sahabuddin's farm in Barorshia field of Shibganj, Durlabpur union, Chapainawabganj. He has cultivated sugarcane on 100 bighas of land in that area through contact farming or contract farming. Due to his initiative, Amrit variety sugarcane is becoming popular in the area. Sahabuddin's ancestral home is in Mubarakpur village of Kharakpur union in Shibganj. He lives with his family in the suburbs of Rajshahi. He completed his studies in Computer Science and Engineering from Chuet in 2018. Then worked in a software company from 2021 to 2023. He left that job and joined his father's business. According to Sahabuddin, he had a weakness for agriculture from childhood. That's when he returned to the village and started working with his father on modern agriculture.

how to start

About the year 2020-21. Sugarcane is cultivated in own land. They make unadulterated molasses - after hearing that, many acquaintances in Dhaka are interested in buying this molasses. At that time, he gave home delivery of large sugarcane molasses (weighing more than 20 kg) among some acquaintances. Those who took it, after tasting the pure jaggery, asked for it again. In this way, he sold 200 kg of jaggery in the first year. Entered service in September 2021. Along with the job, continue selling Jaggery. In 2022, he was introduced to businessmen who market adulterated safe food. They expressed great interest in this jaggery. Many come to the field to collect jaggery. Thus the demand keeps increasing. Due to increased demand, after the sugarcane of father's three bigha land was exhausted, he made molasses with well-known farmers of the area.

Sahabuddin met the scientists of Bangladesh Sugarcrop Research Institute (BSRI) in 2022. They have come out with a powder variant of sugarcane molasses. He remembers seeing it. After training, he started making this powdered jaggery. In 2023, he leased 21 bigha of land. With ancestral three bighas. It cannot meet the demand. Since last year, they have been producing powdered jaggery commercially. Powdered jaggery can be stored for up to two years. Can be kept at home and eaten throughout the year. So got good response from buyers. Last year he sold 35 tonnes of powdered jaggery.

increased production

According to BSRI data, in Shibganj upazila of Chapainawabganj, molasses production and chewing sugarcane cultivation was done in 5 thousand 267 hectares of land in the fiscal year 2022-23. This year cultivation has been done in 6 thousand 5 hectares of land. Recently, the local farmer said. with the king Earlier he cultivated sugarcane in his one bigha land. Now coming to an agreement with Sahabuddin, he is cultivating sugarcane on four bigha land. Md. Badsha said, 'There is no problem in taking our molasses to the market. No need to mix colors. So I am getting a good price.

In the current season, Sahabuddin is making chocolate-sized jaggery with the advice and cooperation of Chittagong honey researcher SM Moinul Anwar. This new jaggery weighs 10 to 12 grams. Can be consumed in one mouthful. This jaggery can be stored in jars. This time, a multinational company bought 1,300 kg chocolate-sized molasses to give Eid gifts to its employees. Honey researcher Moinul Anwar said that during the British period, prisoners were given such small sugarcane molasses to regain energy quickly. From that he got this idea. If the commercial production of this molasses is successful, he wants to name it 'Qaidi' molasses.

A Day at Sahabuddin's Farm

Last month, when I went to Sahabuddin's farm, I saw nectar sugarcane all around. Jaggery is being made in 10 kilns in the sugarcane field. Akhmarai work is going on in three crushing machines. Among them is a modern crushing machine. Bangladesh Sugarcrop Research Institute (BSRI) scientists were also present at Sahabuddin's farm for the trial. These scientists measured and found that 5 percent more juice is available in the modern machine than the conventional crushing machine.

When asked about engineer Sahabuddin's venture with molasses, Director General of BSRI Kabir Uddin Ahmed told Prothom Alo, 'Sahabuddin is a promising businessman. He is marketing our powdered jaggery. The chocolate-shaped jaggery is also beautiful to look at. Kabir Uddin said that there is a shortage of 1.6 million tons of sugar in the country. This deficiency can be met by increasing jaggery production. For this, more entrepreneurs like Sahabuddin need to be developed.
